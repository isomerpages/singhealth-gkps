<p></p>
<hr>
<p>This paper details a quality improvement project at an Acute Medical Unit
aimed at eliminating medication admission errors. Traditional processes,
where doctors prescribed medications and pharmacists corrected them later,
resulted in anomalies for 92% of patients.</p>
<p>To address this, the team applied the Safety-Flow-Quality-Productivity
(SFQP) model of Health Care Systems Engineering. The methodology involved
mapping the system and stakeholder needs using tools like the 4N Chart.
Following baseline measurement and a "5-whys" root-cause analysis, the
workflow was completely redesigned. Emphasising "built-in quality" over
retrospective inspection, the clinical pharmacist was moved to the front
of the admission process. This "Pharmacist Clerking" enabled pharmacists
to take the history, write the chart, and order drugs simultaneously.</p>
<p>This "right-first-time" approach was highly successful, reducing the median
number of anomalies from three to zero and the patient error rate from
92% to 2%. The intervention effectively eliminated the need for downstream
firefighting, creating a safer, "win-win-win" system for patients, doctors,
and nurses</p>
